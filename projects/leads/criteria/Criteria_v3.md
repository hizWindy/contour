## 1. Purpose

Find organisations that are likely to move or refit 1,000 m² or more of office space in the next 24 months, and that suit Contour's model: strategy, design and delivery together. Every lead is researched, scored and backed by evidence. So business development calls a short list of qualified companies, not companies picked by headcount alone.

**The one-line test:** an office-based organisation of about 100+ people in Melbourne, Adelaide or Perth. It is going through a big positive change, or is long overdue for a move. It has no recent fitout and no locked-in route to market that shuts Contour out.

## 2. Ideal client profile

A lead must pass all five filters before it is scored. If a fact is unknown, estimate it, label it as an estimate, and say what the estimate rests on.

**An unknown fact is never a fail (TBC).** Al-khair asked for "not too strict". So the gates are loose and the evidence is strict: every fact still needs a source, but a fact nobody found is estimated and labelled, and the lead goes on.

| # | Filter | Passes if |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Location | An office in Melbourne, Adelaide or Perth (priority), or Sydney or Brisbane (secondary). An overseas or interstate company that announces a new office in one of these cities counts. |
| 2 | Office size | 1,000 m² or more now, or within 24 months. If the floor area isn't known, estimate it at about 10 m² per office-based person. Example: no floor area, 120 staff: "about 1,200 m², estimated from staff". |
| 3 | Headcount | 100+ office-based staff in that city, or a believable path to 100+ within 24 months. Example: 50 staff hiring 100 qualifies. 10 staff hiring 20 doesn't. Count the city's staff, not the global or national total. |
| 4 | Office relevance | Most local staff work in an office, and the business values people working together there. Engineers, consultants, analysts, and project and corporate teams count. Warehouse, plant, retail, field and site staff don't. |
| 5 | Sector | The priority sectors are energy, renewables, critical infrastructure, defence, engineering, insurance, mid-tier financial services, technology, professional services (not legal) and resources head offices. Other private-sector organisations can qualify on strong signs. |

**Behaviour (scored, not a filter).** The organisation is in a big positive change, and would value advice on how much space it needs and how it should work. Signs: fast growth, a merger or acquisition, a new leadership agenda, a market entry, or a workplace or culture change.

**All of Australia (TBC).** Al-khair asked for all of Australia, not only 4 cities. Levi's notes name 5 cities, so the other places are our list:

| Group | Places |
|---|---|
| Priority | Melbourne, Adelaide, Perth |
| Secondary | Sydney, Brisbane |
| Capitals | Canberra, Hobart, Darwin |
| Regional | Gold Coast, Newcastle, Sunshine Coast, Wollongong, Geelong, Townsville, Cairns, Toowoomba, Ballarat, Bendigo, Launceston, Mackay |

- Any place on the list passes the location filter. A place not on the list passes too, with the flag "check the city".
- Sydney is no longer a Director's decision. Levi's notes call it "secondary".

**50+ staff still qualifies (TBC).** Levi said on 9 Nov that 50+ staff fits. His notes now say 100+, or a believable path to 100+. So 50 to 99 staff still passes the headcount filter. Only 100+ earns the full size points (section 6), and 50 to 99 earns 2.

## 3. Exclusions

Left out, not scored. Each goes on its own list with the reason and the evidence, so the team can override it.

| # | Left out | Not left out |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Government departments and agencies | A government-owned corporation or commercialised body: flagged, not excluded |
| 2 | Not-for-profits and charities that rely on grants or donations | A large, well-funded member-owned organisation, like an industry super fund or a member-owned insurer |
| 3 | Law firms, especially tier one and national firms | |
| 4 | The big four banks and tier-one financial institutions that buy design apart from delivery | |
| 5 | Organisations where the office is incidental: logistics, manufacturing, retail or hospitality operators whose local staff are mostly not office staff | |
| 6 | Organisations that buy fitouts only through formal public tender portals, like AusTender, Tenders VIC, Tenders WA and SA Tenders | |

**How we apply this (TBC):**
- An excluded lead is a **Drop**, with its reason and evidence. A person with the Lead Finder tick can override it, and the lead is then judged again without the exclusion. The tool records who did it.
- This replaces version 2's Director review for government, law firms, premium banks and finance, and standard not-for-profits.
- Education, real estate agencies and hospitals aren't in Levi's list. They stay a Director review.
- "Industry consultants" isn't in Levi's list either, so it stays a Director review too. His notes list professional services (not legal) as a priority sector, so it may not need one: a question for him.
- Law firms: the notes say "especially tier one and national firms". We leave out all law firms.
- **Which company is "one"?** The research files each company in an industry group, and the group decides. A company is not left out, and the card flags it, when its industry or its owner says it is: a government-owned or commercialised body (not a department), a member-owned organisation like an industry super fund (not a charity), or an insurer, a fund manager or a customer-owned bank (not a big bank). Insurance and mid-tier financial services are priority sectors in his notes.
- **The office is incidental** only when the research says the office doesn't matter to how the company works, in a site-heavy industry (construction, mining and metals, machinery, manufacturing, food and beverages, retail, automotive, hospitality, transport and logistics, civil engineering). In any other industry that answer is a flag, not a reason to leave the lead out.
- **Tender portals** only count when a note says the company buys fitouts solely through them. A company that uses a portal and other routes is a dampener (section 5).

## 4. Signs (Levi calls them trigger events)

A sign is evidence that a property decision is coming. One strong sign earns a look. Two or more independent signs within 12 months make a strong lead. Record the date and the source of every sign. **Ignore anything older than 18 months, unless it is a lease or tenure date.**

| Strength | Sign | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Very high | Lease ending | Lease signed 6 to 9 years ago, or a known end within 36 months. Building being sold, redeveloped or refurbished. |
| Very high | Long tenure | At the current address 8+ years (10+ is strongest), with no known fitout there since. See "Long tenure" below. |
| High | Hiring surge | Open roles in one city equal to 25%+ of current local headcount, mostly office-based |
| High | Merger or acquisition | Two businesses with offices in the same city combining. Integration or consolidation announced. |
| High | Capital raise (new) | Series B+, private equity, an IPO or a major debt raise, with stated growth plans |
| High | New market entry | An overseas or interstate company announcing an office or hub in Melbourne, Adelaide or Perth |
| High | Major contract or program win | A large defence, energy, infrastructure or government program that needs a project team |
| High | Tenant advisor (new) | The company appoints a tenant representation agent, or appears in leasing news as looking for space |
| Medium | New CEO or C-suite | A new leader with a stated growth, culture or transformation agenda |
| Medium | Restructure or consolidation | Merging several local sites, bringing teams in-house, a new operating model |
| Medium | Workplace policy change | A return-to-office rule, a new hybrid policy, a stated culture or talent problem |
| Medium | Sector tailwind | Government or industry news driving growth across a sector, like renewable energy zones, AUKUS or grid investment. It also starts a competitor check. |
| Medium | Version 2's other signs | New business unit, new facility, underperforming workplace (**TBC**: Levi's notes don't list them) |
| Low | Awards and growth lists (new) | AFR Fast 100, Deloitte Technology Fast 50, industry growth rankings. Low on its own. It supports other signs. |
| Low | Culture change, hiring push, rival growth | Version 2's weak signs (**TBC**: Levi's notes don't list them) |

**Competitor check.** When a qualifying company grows fast, find its three to five closest competitors in the same city and check them for the same signs, even with no news. Growth across a sector often shows first in job ads and LinkedIn headcount, not in headlines.

**Long tenure.** Organisations usually move or refit about every 10 years. A company at its current address for 8+ years is a lead even without fast growth, if all of these hold:
- it meets the size and office filters;
- it is stable or growing (no layoffs, losses or contraction);
- it has no known fitout at that address in the last 5 years.

Tenure is a very high sign on its own. Any growth sign on top makes it a strong lead. An exact move-in date isn't needed. Evidence that the company has been at the address for at least 8 years is enough. Write it as "at this address since at least 2017".

**How we apply this (TBC):**
- **Search signs.** A run searches the main signs by default. They are now 8: lease ending, merger or acquisition, big contract, fast growth, new market entry, sector tailwind, capital raise and tenant advisor. Long tenure has no search: the code adds it from the office dates. Version 2 searched 6, so each city's run does 2 more searches.
- **18 months.** A sign counts if it happened in the last 18 months. It was 12. A lease sign counts while the lease end is up to 3 years away. A sign that is 12 to 18 months old counts, but earns no recency points.
- **Long tenure** needs a fitout longer ago than 5 years, or none known. In version 2 any fitout after the move-in stopped it. A company that is cutting staff fast or in administration is not stable, so it gets no tenure sign.
- **The competitor check** runs for a good lead in a priority sector, and now for a good lead with a sector tailwind sign, whatever its sector.
- **Signs his notes don't list** keep their version 2 groups and get the strength we judged: new business unit, new facility and underperforming workplace are medium. Culture change, hiring push and rival growth are low.

## 5. Dampeners

A dampener lowers a lead's score. It never removes the lead. Search for each one on purpose, because a missed dampener gives a false lead.

| Dampener | How to check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A fitout or move in the target city in the last 3 years | Fitout and design firms' project pages, commercial property news, lease deal reports, company announcements, LinkedIn posts about a new office | Strong. Still list the lead if hiring growth would outgrow the space within 24 months. |
| A fitout or move 3 to 5 years ago | The same | Moderate |
| A global or national real estate outsourcing deal | Press releases and case studies from JLL, CBRE, Cushman & Wakefield, Colliers and similar that name the company as a client | Strong |
| A known tie to a competitor | A design-and-build firm or architect has delivered 2 or more of its projects, or holds a panel or global agreement | Moderate |
| Procurement red flags | A formal tender portal for fitouts, offshore or central global buying, a history of lowest-price tenders, design and build bought apart | Moderate to strong |
| Financial stress | Layoffs, profit warnings, administration or a hiring freeze | Strong, unless it is part of a consolidation that creates a move |

- When one is found, record the evidence and the date, and say whether anything could reverse it. Examples: an agreement's renewal date, or a new CEO who may change suppliers.
- **"Not found" is not "none".** The first four rows are checked on every lead that passes the filters. Having no evidence is written as "not found".
- **TBC, our reading of "moderate to strong".** A tender portal, or offshore or central global buying, is strong. A lowest-price history, or design and build bought apart, is moderate.
- **TBC, financial stress.** In version 2, a company in administration or cutting staff fast was a Drop. Levi's notes make it a dampener, so it is now a strong one. The old Drop stays until the new score is built.

**How we apply this (TBC):**
- **Five dampeners, six rows.** The two fitout rows are one dampener with two strengths: strong up to 3 years ago, moderate from 3 to 5 years. A fitout longer ago than 5 years doesn't count.
- **Every lead lists all five.** Each is **found**, **not found** (the check ran and showed nothing) or **not checked** (nobody could check: a lead made before this rule, or a search that was skipped). A check that is not checked says nothing either way.
- **The search.** The research has a fourth angle, `dampeners`, with up to 3 web searches a company. They are on top of the 12 the Settings page allows, so a company may use 3 more. The money limits are the same. Claude answers each of the four checks `found` or `not_found`, and a found one needs a page we read. Without one, it goes back to "not checked".
- **Facts we already hold count too.** The move-in date and the last fitout feed the first dampener, and a company that buys fitouts only through tender portals feeds procurement. So a card never says "not found" beside a fitout it shows elsewhere.
- **A fitout with no date** counts as moderate, and says "date not known".
- **Outgrow rule.** A found dampener takes nothing off when the company plans to hire a lot, says it will need more space than it has now, and needs it within 24 months. The card still shows it as found, with the reason. This is our reading of "still list the lead".
- **Consolidation.** Financial stress takes nothing off when the company has a merger or takeover sign, or an office consolidation sign.
- **Not built yet.** The points (20 off for strong, 10 for moderate) come with the new score (T1-6). Until then a dampener is shown and doesn't change the result.

## 6. Scoring

Each lead that passes the filters is scored out of 100. A separate confidence rating goes beside it.

| Part | Check | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Fit (30) | Local office headcount and projected size: 100 staff = 5, 150+ = 10, 250+ = 12. **TBC:** 50 to 99 = 2. | 12 |
| | Priority sector | 8 |
| | City: Melbourne, Adelaide, Perth = 5. Sydney, Brisbane = 2. **TBC:** any other place = 1. | 5 |
| | Office relevance and a work-together culture | 5 |
| Change and timing (50) | The strongest sign: very high = 25, high = 18, medium = 10, low = 4 | 25 |
| | Other independent signs within 12 months: 8 each, at most 2 | 16 |
| | The current office is constrained or ending: 8+ years at the address, lease timing, overflow space, split sites | 9 |
| Access (20) | No outsourcing deal or competitor lock-in found | 10 |
| | Favourable procurement: direct engagement, values strategy, past integrated delivery | 6 |
| | A warm path in: an existing Contour contact, a shared board member, an agent or advisor tie | 4 |

**Then the dampeners.** Take off 20 for a strong one and 10 for a moderate one.

| Tier | Score | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A: pursue now | 75+ | Personal outreach within two weeks |
| B: nurture | 55 to 74 | Add to a campaign. Check again monthly. |
| C: watch | 40 to 54 | Watch for a new sign |
| Discard | Under 40 | Don't list |

**Confidence: high, medium or low.**
- **High:** the headcount, the signs and the dampener checks all have named sources.
- **Low:** key facts are estimated.
- A high score with low confidence gets the flag "verify before outreach" and goes to a person before anyone reaches out.
- A fact that Property Daily or Arealytics confirms counts as confirmed.

**How the tiers become the four results (TBC).** The review page, the goal and the HubSpot stages already use four results, so they stay:

| Tier | Result |
|---|---|
| A or B | **Pass**, or **Director review** when a Director rule applies |
| C | **Watch** |
| Discard, and any exclusion | **Drop** |

The Director rules that stay: an office over 6,000 m² or over 600 staff (a possible Whale Pipeline project), and the Director industries left after section 3. A good lead is Tier A or B (**TBC**). The goal of 100 good leads every 2 weeks counts those.

**Priority sectors** replace version 2's tier 1 and tier 2 industries. They are the list in section 2.

## 7. Sources to search

On every run, search each source group, and record which source supports each fact.

| Group | Where |
|---|---|
| Mergers and acquisitions | ACCC acquisitions register (mandatory for qualifying deals since 1 Jan 2026), ASX announcements, AFR Street Talk, FIRB-related coverage |
| Funding and growth | AFR, The Australian business section, SmartCompany, Startup Daily, Crunchbase, AFR Fast 100, Deloitte Fast 50 |
| Hiring | SEEK, LinkedIn Jobs, company careers pages, LinkedIn employee counts and 6 to 12 month growth trends |
| Leases, moves and tenure | Commercial property news (The Urban Developer, AFR Commercial Real Estate, agent leasing reports), building redevelopment approvals, landlord leasing announcements. For tenure, see below. |
| Recent fitouts | Project pages of fitout, design and architecture firms. Inside, ArchitectureAU and Office Snapshots. LinkedIn posts about new offices. |
| Outsourcing and competitor ties | JLL, CBRE, Cushman & Wakefield and Colliers client announcements and case studies. Competitor project pages. |
| Leadership change | ASX announcements, LinkedIn, trade press, company media releases |
| Sector growth | Industry journals (energy, defence, infrastructure, insurance), government program and budget news, the Infrastructure Australia pipeline, defence industry news |
| Procurement | AusTender and the state tender portals (as a red-flag check), annual reports, supplier codes of conduct |
| Company facts | Annual reports, ASIC records, ASX filings, company websites, Workplace Gender Equality Agency data (it reports headcount for organisations with 100+ staff) |

**Tenure search.** Search "[company] [address]" and "how long has [company] been at [address]". Then check annual reports and ASIC filings across several years, old copies of the company's contact page (the Wayback Machine), and older news or LinkedIn posts that name the address.

## 8. Research method

Run four passes. Say plainly what couldn't be checked. Accuracy matters more than volume: 10 well-evidenced leads beat 50 guesses.

1. **Discover.** Scan the sign sources for the target cities and priority sectors, then run the competitor check on every strong lead. Separately, check how long every company that passes the size filter has been at its address, so long-tenure leads show up even with no news.
2. **Qualify.** Apply the five filters and the exclusions. Drop what fails, and log why.
3. **Check dampeners.** On every lead that passes, search on purpose for a recent fitout, an outsourcing deal, a competitor tie and procurement red flags. No evidence is written as "not found", never "none".
4. **Score and rank.** Apply the scoring model and the confidence rating. Sort by tier, then score.

**Accuracy rules:**
- Cite a source and a date for every fact. Never invent a headcount, a lease date, a tenure or a contact.
- Keep confirmed facts apart from estimates, and show the working for each estimate.
- Use city figures. A company with 5,000 staff and 60 in Adelaide is a 60-person lead in Adelaide.
- Check the lead list, the client list and past pursuits before adding a company. A repeat run updates a lead and doesn't duplicate it.
- Score a lead again when a new sign or dampener appears, and say what changed since the last run.

## 9. Output

Each run returns a ranked table of leads, then a one-page profile for every Tier A lead, then the excluded list and the watch list. Each lead shows:

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| Company and city | Legal name, trading name, and the address of the target city's office if known |
| Tier, score, confidence | Like "A · 82 · Medium" |
| Sector | One line |
| Local headcount | Now and projected, each marked confirmed or estimated, with its source |
| Space needed | Now and projected, at about 10 m² a person |
| Signs | Each sign with its date and source link |
| Dampeners | Each one found, or "not found" for each of the four checks |
| Current office | Address, building, years at the address with evidence (like "at this address since at least 2017"), the lease date if known, and the known fitout firm |
| Why now | Two sentences on why a property decision is likely in the next 24 months |
| Contour angle | The strategic hook, like post-merger integration, growth planning, consolidating sites, a long overdue refresh, or return to office |
| Who to contact | The likely roles (CEO, COO, CFO, Head of People, Head of Property), and named people only where they are publicly listed |
| Suggested opener | One line the BD team can adapt, tied to the sign |
| What changed | New since the last run, upgraded, downgraded or unchanged |
